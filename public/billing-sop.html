Chapter 01

You own the money side

PRSFlo is two worlds. Operations is the studio manager's: the building, the runners, the nightly checklists. Billing is yours: work orders, invoices, payments, money owed. The two meet in one place, every morning: a work order a runner did not submit. That one is yours to chase.

The one rule that shapes everything

PRSFlo owns the workflow and the documents. QuickBooks owns the money.?Quick test: "where is this invoice, and what is it waiting for?" is a PRSFlo question. "How much did we make in July?" is a QuickBooks question. PRSFlo tells you what needs invoicing, keeps the work order and the invoice PDF together, and always knows where every invoice sits. The invoice itself is still made in QuickBooks, and the books still live there.

Why it worksThe two systems track different things, so they never fight. PRSFlo is right about where things are. QuickBooks is right about the money.

COD clients

Pay at the desk, during or right after the session. The runner records the payment on the work order. Your part: check the work order, attach the QuickBooks invoice, and an owner signs it off. If money was not collected, the session lands in Balance due, the one place COD goes wrong.

Billing clients

Labels, and anyone invoiced after the fact. The full cycle: review → invoice → owner approval → PO → send → chase → paid. Days to weeks per invoice, and the hub tracks every one of them for you.

Where you'll live

Everything is in the left rail.

DashboardThe morning read. Flo's lines at the top, your duties and tasks in Your list, and four money tiles that jump to the hub.
Billing ▾A drop-down with four pages: Billing Hub (every work order and invoice, sorted by what it is waiting for), Petty Cash, SRS, and Client AP Protocols (how each label wants its invoices sent).
Daily OpsLast night, studio by studio. Your part is the red WO not submitted badges. Flags live on the second tab of the same page.
CalendarWhere sessions are booked. Clicking any session opens its work order, the same one the hub opens.

The next chapter follows one session all the way through them.

Chapter 02 · The flow

A session, start to finish

This is why the app is called Flo. Every session moves down one line, from first hold to money in the bank, and every screen you use is a window onto a different part of that line. Learn this chapter and the rest of the manual is detail. This is the Billing client's path; COD's shorter path is chapter 05.

1
Front desk / management · Calendar

It starts with a session, and the work order is born with it

A lead becomes a hold, a hold becomes a session on the calendar. Every session gets one work order, created automatically, with its own number. Nobody makes one by hand. The work order is the session's paperwork: the calendar is just where it shows its dates.

In the hub, a session whose first day is still in the future waits in Not started. From its first day it sits in In progress.

The office · the work order

Set it up before it runs

Open the work order and make sure the office's half is right: the client is on file, each day has its room, times and rate, the staff line has a rate if an engineer is being charged, and for a label the PO # is typed in, or Not req'd is switched on.

A confirmed session will not save without a start and end time on every day and a client on file. A hold can save with just a name.

Runners · their phones, live

The session runs, and the runner submits the night

The runner works the same work order from their phone: equipment, rentals, the song titles, and when the client really Arrived and Left. If the artist never came, they tap No show instead. Then they press Submit for that night. They cannot submit without Arrived and Left, or No show.

Arrived / Left and No show are a record only. They never change the bill. Billed stays as booked.

Runners can submit at any time, including the next morning when a session runs late. Until they do, the office sees that night as Not submitted.

Chapter 03

The work order

One document per session, and the single source of truth for it. The calendar card, the runner's phone, the hub row and the PDF the client receives are all windows onto this one document. On a computer it is two columns: words on the left (who, what kind, notes), numbers on the right (days, rentals, payments, totals). This chapter goes through it part by part.

The top bar: Cancel · Complete WO · Save

SaveSaves and closes. Most of what you type is only on your screen until you press it.
CancelCloses without saving, with no question asked. It removes any day you added since opening and puts back, whole, any day you deleted.
Complete WO"I checked this and it is right." It saves, records your name, and moves the work order to Needs invoice in the hub. It will not complete if any day is missing its times (lockouts excepted). Afterwards the button reads Reopen WO, which asks first and takes it back out of the billing queue.
A few things save the moment you touch themand Cancel will not undo them: Mark reviewed, food expenses and their receipt photos, the equipment pills, the SRS tick, the × that clears a room's staff line, and days added with Seed. Press Save before you use Seed: it reloads every day and drops edits you had not saved. If you click outside the work order with unsaved changes, it asks: Save and close, Discard my changes, or Keep editing.

The letterhead

The WO number, the Invoice # under it, the status badge, and ⟲ HISTORY.

History shows who changed what and when, and the original work order as it was first created. It records changes to the client, status, PO, days, times, rates, staff and notes, every "Day submitted" and "Reviewed the day", and the owner's approve or return.

It does not record payments, rentals, food receipts, the discount, Complete / Reopen, or the hub's Mark sent, Mark paid, Pull it back and Close. For those, what is on the page is the record.

The client card

Who the session is for. Search for the client, the A&R or the artist. Picking an artist fills the artist, their A&R and the label. Picking an A&R fills the A&R and the label. Picking a label fills only the label.

A client we have never worked with gets a new card: a first and last name for a COD client, the label or company name for a Billing one, and a phone or an email. A session with only a typed name shows Not on file with Put on file →.

The COD / Label/Billing switch is the big one for you. It decides which side of the hub this work order lives on, and whether the 3% card fee applies. Picking a label sets it to Label/Billing for you.

Status: for the session, and for each day

The status bar has eight pills: Confirmed, Tentative, Cancelled, Lockout, and four that are calendar blocks with no billing at all: Tour, Tech, Open Hrs and Tenant.

On a multi-day session each day has its own pill (Confirmed / Tentative / Cancelled). When the days differ, the top bar reads Mixed. Tapping a pill on the top bar sets every day at once.

A cancelled dayStays on the work order, struck through, and reads Not billed. It adds nothing to the total.
A cancelled sessionStill bills in full and still comes to you in the hub. If it is a kill fee, put it in as a Discount with the reason. If nothing is owed, close the invoice from the hub with a reason (chapter 04).

Studio Time: one card per day

This is the only place the schedule and the staffing live. Each card shows the room, the date, the booked times, the staff, and the day's total. Click a card to open that day and edit it. The day's own Save only keeps the edit on screen; the top-bar Save is what stores it.

Adding days+ Add studio time asks First day, Last day and Studio. New days copy the times and rate type from the last day. + Seed adds several rooms or a long run in one go, and it saves immediately.
Moving a dayUse the − date + chip inside the day. Its rooms, staff and rate move with it.
Deleting one dayThe × on the day's card, then Delete. This is how you remove one day. It is not kept in the Deleted work orders log, so be sure.
Times TBDA TBD chip beside Start / End says "not decided yet" on purpose. A blank is an oversight; a chip is a decision. Typing a time clears it.
Two rooms, one dayOne block per room inside the same day, each with its own rate. The calendar shows a card in each room.

Rates: by the hour or by the day

Each room on each day is priced / HR or / DAY. A day rate is always ten times the hourly rate, and switching converts it for you.

Hourly rows have no overtime. They bill the booked hours, and a longer session is simply more hours.

Day rows include a set number of hours: the Hours included box, blank meaning 12. Past that, overtime is charged at the OT rate / hr beside it, which fills in as the day rate ÷ 10 and can be changed.

A new room's rate fills in from Admin → Rates when there is one. It never overwrites a rate somebody typed. A missing rate shows an orange rate? on the card.

Staff: 1ST and 2ND

Every day starts with one staff line. The chip toggles between 1ST (engineer) and 2ND (assistant). A name is optional: "Engineer · TBD" is valid. A second room added to the same day comes with no staff of its own.

The charge is the staff hours × their rate. No rate means no charge. A named engineer with no rate shows rate? so it cannot be missed.

Staff times follow the studio times until you change them. + Add engineer / + Add assistant inside a day adds a second person.

Whole-building price

The Blanket rate switch sets one price for all of that day's rooms. The price is shared out across the rooms for you, and overtime is typed as an amount.

What the runner sent, and your review

Submitted · name · timeThe runner pressed Submit for that night. They can still correct it until you review it.
Not submittedThe night has passed and nobody submitted it. This is the red flag in the hub and the badge on Daily Ops.
Mark reviewedYour check on one day. It becomes ✓ Reviewed and locks that day: the runner sees "Approved by the office — locked" and can no longer edit or submit it. If the night is over and the runner never submitted it, your review submits it in your name. Tap again to reopen the day (that does not un-submit it). You can still edit a reviewed day yourself.
Client actually hereArrived / Left, or the red No show chip. A record of what really happened, for our own use. No charge is worked out from it.
Three things clear a "never submitted" flagThe runner submits late, you Mark reviewed that day, or someone presses Complete WO. Invoicing, sending and getting paid do not clear it. The flag does not care about status: a cancelled session or an old hold whose date has passed gets it too, and is cleared the same way.

Rentals

Item · Supplier · Date(s) Used · Qty · Rate · Charge. Charge fills in as Qty × Rate. You can type over Charge; it holds until the Qty or Rate changes.

Food

The Food budget pill turns it on. The bubble beside it opens the expense report: the budget, each receipt (place, amount including tip, a photo), what is left.

The client is billed receipts + a service fee. The fee is 45% unless you change the % on that line for this work order. It appears in the totals as its own Food line.

Discount

One line in the totals: % or $, a value, and a reason. It comes off studio time and engineering only, never rentals, food or card fees.

This is how a kill fee is done: leave the cancelled session billing in full and discount it down, with the reason typed in.

The notes, and who sees them

Booking Notes — internal. Never on the invoice.
Session Notes — the client-facing note.
Needs Attention — internal. Never on the PDF.
Song title / session info — one line per day. Some labels will not pay without it.

Payments and the totals

The Payments section opens with a COLLECT box. On a Billing client it says the amount is invoiced and there is nothing to collect at the desk. On COD it shows two numbers the same size: cash / Zelle / check, and card including the 3% fee. Chapter 05 covers taking a COD payment properly.

Below it, the totals, every one worked out from the rows above and never typed:

Studio Total + Eng Total + Rentals Total + Food = Subtotal
− Discount + Card Fees (3%) = Grand Total
− Total Paid = Balance Due

If a number looks wrongdo not argue with the total. Read the rows: the day cards, the rentals, the payments. The answer is always in them, and the hub row shows the same figures.

Deleting a work order, and getting it back

A session's work order can be deleted in one place only: its row's ⋯ in the hub. You, the manager and the owners can do it. The work order itself has no Delete button (calendar blocks like a Tour still do). A Closed row with no invoice on it has no ⋯; Reopen it first.

It removes the whole work order: every day on it and every calendar card. You have to type the WO number to confirm. To remove just one day, delete that day inside the work order instead.

Everything deleted is kept. The ⋯ at the top right of the page → Deleted work orders lists what was deleted, by whom and when. Recover → Put it back restores it exactly as it was, same WO number, and writes a "Recovered" line into its history.

Also under the page ⋯"Generate WO" downloads a blank work order form to print. It creates nothing in the app. If the session is real, it needs a real work order too.

Guardrails you'll notice (they're features)

Changing the money after approval un-approves it. An owner approved $4,180, not "whatever this becomes". Edit the numbers and the row goes back to Needs approval, with Changed since invoiced showing until the owner approves the corrected invoice. Swapping the PDF or adding a PO does not un-approve.

A session that grows goes back. If the client extends and new nights are added to an invoiced, unsent work order, the row reads Needs invoice again (even though an invoice is on it) until those nights have been submitted or the last day has passed. Then it returns to Needs approval; re-issue the invoice for the new total.

A sent invoice never silently changes. Once it is in Awaiting payment the only way to restart it is Pull it back.

Overdue is counted from sent. The 31 days run from the day you pressed Mark sent, never from the session date.

The hub is where state changes are made, not where they are logged. Mark sent, Mark paid, Pull it back and Close leave no line in the work order's history. If one of them matters later, put a line in ✎ collection notes when you do it.

Chapter 05

COD

COD is paid at the desk, at the top of the session. When that happens, your part takes minutes. The only way COD goes wrong is a balance nobody collected, and the whole COD side of the hub is built to make that impossible to miss.

Balance due · 1In progressNeeds review · 3PaidNot started

Click COD at the top of the hub. These tabs are switches, not pages: more than one can be on at once, and the list shows them together. It opens with Balance due and Needs review on, which is your COD morning.

Balance dueThe session has happened and the charges are more than the payments. The row says Record payment on the WO. There is no button: collect the money, add the payment on the work order, and the row moves by itself.
Needs reviewPaid up, no invoice on it yet. Check the work order, Complete WO, drop the QuickBooks invoice on the row.
Needs approvalPaid and invoiced. COD needs an owner's sign-off too; it waits in the Paid tab until they approve.
PaidPaid up and approved by an owner. Finished. The row keeps a Download button for building the package; on COD you rarely need it.

COD has no Mark sent and no Mark paid. Paid is worked out from the payments on the work order, so it cannot be marked paid when it is not.

Taking a payment: the COLLECT box

On a COD work order the Payments section opens with COLLECT: two numbers, the same size.

$410.00 · cash · Zelle · check$422.30 · card · incl. 3% fee $12.30

Quote the client the number for how they are paying. Then:

  1. Press + Add payment and pick the type. The list shows the amount beside each type, and picking one fills the row in for the full balance.
  2. For a part payment or a deposit, type the amount in Toward balance.
  3. On a card, the row adds the fee and shows the total in red: $123.00 + $3.69 fee = $126.69. The red number is what you run on the terminal.
  4. Type the card's last 4, then Save.
Toward balance is the amount BEFORE the feeNever type what the terminal charged into that box, or the 3% goes on twice. If you do, a red line appears under the payment ("Is $126.69 what ran on the card?") with a Use $123.00 link that fixes it in one tap. It catches the common cases, not every one, and it never blocks. A "paid" figure higher than the total is the sign it was missed.

The 3% card fee

Applies to Credit Card and Debit Card on COD work orders only. Cash, Zelle, Venmo, Wire, ACH, Check and Other carry no fee. Billing clients never carry it.

It belongs to the payment, not the invoice, and shows in the totals as Card Fees (3%). The waive link on a payment removes the fee from that payment.

From the row's ⋯ on COD

Export the work order PDF"For the client, before approval." The work order on its own, with the food expense table and receipts, for a client who wants paperwork on the night. No invoice in it.
Add a PO numberFor the rare COD client whose company wants one on the paperwork.
Close this invoiceThe same seven reasons as Billing. This is how a COD balance that will never be collected leaves Balance due, with the reason on the record.

Duties are the recurring part of the job: daily, weekly on set days, or monthly on a date. Tasks are one-offs assigned to you. Click a duty to tick it; click again to untick. Click a task to open it on the Flags tab.

Click the box itself to open the full list. There you can see every recurring item, and add your own (repeating or one-off). Items Eli set carry a 🔒 and cannot be edited or removed.

This list is the source of truth for your dutiesThis manual does not name them, because they can change without the app changing. If it is on Your list today, it is due today.

The rules the list lives by

Weekdays only. Daily duties are due Monday to Friday. On a weekend they show as "not due". Monday covers the weekend's work without counting it as missed.

Day-scoped duties only show on their day. A Monday duty shows on Monday; a duty for the 25th shows on the 25th. Do not count on a reminder the day before.

Missed days add up, honestly. For the duties set up that way, a missed day does not duplicate. One red line says how many days it covers, and one tick clears the whole backlog. Items you add yourself never go red; they simply wait.

Monthly duties stay until done. Miss one and it stays on the list, red, counting the days late.

Never late on the day itself. At 9 AM, today's unticked duties are just today's work. Late means an earlier day went unfinished.

"Last night: a work order was never submitted." Once a day, if any of last night's sessions was not submitted. It lists each one with the room, the client and who closed that studio. A memo. Announcements from Eli or the manager open on the dashboard to read and, if asked, sign with your initials. You can put one off once; 48 hours later it comes back and stays until you sign it. They are all kept under Memos in the rail.

What clearing something means

A duty lineclears when you tick the duty on Your list. One tick covers the whole backlog it names. There is no dismiss and no snooze, on purpose.
A money or data lineclears itself when reality changes. Collect the balance and that line goes; review the work orders and that one goes. You never tick anything.
The mental modelFlo's lines are a mirror, not an inbox. Nothing in them needs "processing", and a line disappears when the thing it describes stops being true.

Flo is a summary; the hub is the count. Flo shows only the first few red lines, so on a bad morning the COD line can be pushed off. And its COD figure is not quite the hub's Balance due: it looks back 90 days and includes sessions still running tonight. When the two differ, the hub's Balance due is the one to work from.

The written briefing

Full briefing → opens it. It also opens by itself once a day, the first time you visit the dashboard after it has been written. It has a shared read of the building and a For you section for the Billing seat.

Brief me now → asks Flo to write a fresh one on the spot (it reads Re-brief ↻ once one exists). Some lines end in a small link (notes →, flags →, calendar →, crm →) that takes you to the thing it is talking about.

It is written from notes people typed, so it can be incomplete or out of date. When it and the hub disagree about money, the hub is right.

Daily Ops & Flags

Daily Ops is mostly the studio manager's page: did last night's opening and closing duties come in. One thing on it is yours, and it is the first stop of your morning: the runners who did not submit a work order.

The page

Rail → Daily Ops. It opens on Yesterday; the arrows step back and forward a day. Four cards, one per studio, all the same size. On the left of each card are the opener's and closer's duties with a dot beside each: green came in, amber came in with a problem, red never came in. Those dots are the manager's.

At the top there are two titles: Daily Ops and Flags.

Yours: the red "WO not submitted" badge

WO not submitted · PRS B · Hitline RecordsTL

It sits at the top of the right side of a studio's card. It shows the room, the client, and the initials of the runner who had it: the last runner to touch that work order that night, or failing that whoever filed the closing checklist. A ? means nobody touched it at all.

  1. Tap the badge to open the work order and see what is missing.
  2. Talk to the runner. They can still submit it themselves, even the next day. If they cannot, get the facts and fix the work order yourself, then Mark reviewed.
  3. Tick the round check on the badge. It turns grey; hover it to see "Handled" and who ticked it. Everyone in the office sees the same tick.
What happens to the badge afterwardsIf the runner submits late, the badge goes away. If the office fixes it (Mark reviewed, which submits it in your name, or Complete WO), the badge stays on that night's card as the record that it was missed and who had it, and your tick is what says it was dealt with.

The second title on the same page. A flag is anything that needs doing, and a task is simply a flag somebody typed. Each has a kind, and the kind decides who gets it:

Chapter 11

Help & words

When something looks wrong

A number that can't be right — open the work order and read the rows. Every total is worked out from them.

"Paid" is more than the total, or the balance is negative — almost always a card payment where the terminal amount was typed into Toward balance. Open the work order; the red line under the payment offers the fix.

A row will not open — the message says why. If it says the session was deleted, look in the page ⋯ → Deleted work orders before doing anything else.

A work order has vanished — check Deleted work orders first, then tell Eli. Gaps in the WO numbers do not mean anything was deleted; numbers are sometimes skipped.

You pressed Mark sent or Mark paid by mistake — the only undo is ⋯ → Pull it back, which also removes the invoice and the approval. If it says only an owner can, ask Eli or Adam-Mike.

You dropped a PDF on the wrong row — that row's ⋯ → Remove the attached invoice. Then drop it where it belongs.

A red "NOT saved" message — the save failed and the app is telling you honestly. Try again; if it keeps happening, tell Eli. Errors are logged and he can see them.

The manual doesn't match the screen — the app changed and the manual didn't. Say so. What changed in each release is under Training → App guide (SOP) → Version History.
