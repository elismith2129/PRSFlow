How the timers work
Every Hot and Warm lead has a countdown timer running in the background. The timer controls when a lead gets demoted. Logging a touch does not reset it. Only clicking Keep Hot? or Keep Warm? resets it.
🔥 HOT TIMER — 5 days
Lead created as Hot → 5-day timer starts
Day 5 arrives → Keep Hot? button appears on the card
Click Keep Hot? → timer resets, lead stays Hot for another 5 days
Don't click it → lead auto-demotes to Warm immediately
☀ WARM TIMER — 3 days
Lead demoted to Warm → 3-day timer starts
Day 3 arrives → Keep Warm? button appears on the card
Click Keep Warm? → timer resets for another 3 days
Don't click it → lead auto-demotes to Cold/Dead
✓
8am daily reset — every morning, all Hot and Warm leads reappear in Needs Action. That's your daily call list.
✓
Contact ≠ timer reset — logging a contact removes the lead from Needs Action for the day but the demotion timer keeps counting. Only Keep Hot? / Keep Warm? resets the clock.
✗
Don't ignore Keep Hot? / Keep Warm? — if you see that button and don't click it, the lead demotes automatically. If they're still worth pursuing, click it.
Working the Needs Action view
Needs Action is your daily call list. Every morning at 8am, every Hot and Warm lead reappears here. Work through it top to bottom — Uncontacted first, always: contact brand-new inquiries the same day, and escalate to upper management if you can't handle one. Then work your Hot leads. Each lead has a Contact button. Here's exactly what happens when you click it.
1
Click Contact on a lead
An inline prompt expands below the lead card. You don't leave the page — it all happens inline.
2
Select contact method
Choose how you reached them: Call · Text · Email. This gets logged in the notes as [5/21 2:30pm] ES - Call
3
Update temperature (optional)
If the lead's status changed based on the conversation, update it here: Hot · Warm · Cold/Dead. Changing status resets the demotion timer. Leave it if nothing changed.
4
Add notes (optional)
Any context worth capturing — what they said, next steps, anything relevant. Keep it brief and specific. This is what the next person reads.
5
Click Log Contact
Lead disappears from Needs Action. It'll reappear tomorrow at 8am if it's still Hot or Warm. The demotion timer keeps running in the background.
UNCONTACTED leads
Work these first, every time. A fresh inquiry sitting uncontacted is the most expensive thing in the CRM. Same-day contact, no exceptions. Escalate to management immediately if you can't handle it.
HOT leads
Work these right after uncontacted. A HOT lead sitting untouched in Needs Action is money walking out the door. Same-day contact, no exceptions.
WARM leads
Work these after HOT is cleared. Consistent presence is what moves a Warm lead toward booking. Keep it personal — don't just check a box.
Keep Hot? / Keep Warm?
If this button appears on a card, the demotion timer has expired. Click it to reset the timer and keep them at their current status. Ignore it and they demote automatically.

Training Guide · Clients
A booked lead becomes
a client for life.
The Clients page is the permanent record for everyone who books with Paramount. ID, agreements, contact info, history — it all lives here. A session should never happen without a full profile on file.
How a client profile gets created
01
Book in CRM
Lead confirms. Click Book Client → New Client. A unique registration link is generated.
02
Client Registers
Send the link. The client fills out their own profile, uploads ID, and signs T&Cs. You don't enter this for them.
03
Review Profile
Check immediately. ID uploaded? T&Cs signed? Phone and email present? Missing anything — flag it now.
Managing client profiles
Golden rules
✓ Always
✓Verify ID is uploaded before any session
✓Confirm T&Cs are signed
✓Update contact info the moment it changes
✓Add notes after significant interactions
✓Flag incomplete profiles immediately
✗ Never
✗Allow a session without ID on file
✗Fill out a client's registration for them
✗Delete a record without Manager approval
✗Ignore an incomplete profile
✗Let outdated contact info sit
Training Guide · Tasks
Assignments that get done.
Not problems to fix.
Tasks are action items between staff — "do this thing." Round up tenants late on rent, have a runner clean the patio, order new mic stands. If something is broken or wrong, that's a Flag, not a Task. And if it's not in the system, it doesn't exist.
What a task is — and what it isn't
✓ Tasks are
✓Action items and assignments between staff
✓"Round up tenants late on rent"
✓"Have a runner clean the patio"
✓"Order new mic stands"
✗ Tasks are NOT
✗Operational problems — those go in Flags
✗A broken alarm
✗A client who left without paying
✗A facility issue
Who sees what — visibility rules
Tasks aren't a global broadcast. Visibility is scoped so people see what's theirs — plus the people who need the full picture. Understand this before you assign a task or go looking for one.
You
You see tasks you created and tasks assigned to you.
Managers & Owners
Managers and owners see all tasks across the team.
Comments
Comments follow the task — if you can see the task, you can see and add to the thread.
Creating a task
A good task is specific enough that anyone could pick it up and know exactly what "done" looks like. Here's the flow.
1
Click + on the Tasks panel
Opens the new-task form right there on the dashboard. No separate screen.
2
Write a clear, specific description
Say exactly what needs to happen. "Clean the patio before the 6pm session," not "clean." The person doing it should never have to guess.
3
Assign it to a person or role
Pick who owns it. An unassigned task is a task nobody's doing.
4
Add a photo if relevant
Optional, but a picture often says it faster — the shelf to fix, the area to clean, the part to order.
5
Submit
The task lands with the assignee and appears for everyone who can see it. It's now in the system — which means it's real.
Completing a task
When the work is done, close it out. A finished task left open is just noise on everyone's list.
1
Open the task
Tap it to open the full ticket and its comment thread.
2
Add a comment if there's anything to note
What you did, anything that came up, a photo of the result. Optional — but it's how the next person knows the story.
3
Mark complete
Closes the task and clears it off the active list. Done means marked done.
Golden rules
✓ Always
✓Put it in the system — if it's not in the system, it doesn't exist
✓Be specific — "clean patio," not "clean"
✓Assign every task to someone
✓Complete tasks the moment they're done
✗ Never
✗Leave finished work marked incomplete
✗Let tasks hang open with no movement
✗Use a Task for something broken — raise a Flag
✗Assign a vague, one-word task
Training Guide · Flags
Something is wrong.
Flag it.
Flags are operational issues that need attention or resolution — the alarm won't set, a client left without paying, the vines at Encore need trimming, a mic stand is broken. Flags are not to-dos between staff; those are Tasks. Raise a flag the moment you spot the problem.
What a flag is — and what it isn't
✓ Flags are
✓Operational issues that need attention or resolution
✓"Alarm won't set"
✓"Client left without paying"
✓"Need to trim the vines at Encore"
✓"Mic stand broken"
✗ Flags are NOT
✗Action items or to-dos between staff
✗"Order new mic stands" — that's a Task
✗An assignment for someone to go do
The three categories
Facility / General
Physical space issues, access, maintenance — anything related to the building or the grounds.
Gear / Equipment
Broken or missing equipment — mic stands, cables, outboard gear.
Client / Billing
Client-related issues — payment problems, disputes, conduct.
How runners report flags
Runners are the eyes on the ground — most flags start with them. A flag can be raised from three places:
1 · Checklist
Mark an item as needs attention while working an opening or closing checklist.
2 · Work Order
Mark needs attention on a piece of equipment directly on the Work Order.
3 · Manually
Raise a flag by hand straight from the dashboard when something comes up.
💡 Writing the runner note
Describe the issue clearly and be specific — location, what happened, what it looks like. "Mic stand in Studio B, boom arm won't hold, drops on its own." A photo helps a lot.
How managers handle flags
When a flag comes in, work it end to end. Don't just read it and move on — categorize it, acknowledge it, and resolve it with real detail.
1
Open it — read the runner note
Understand what happened before you do anything else. The runner note is your starting point.
2
Select a category
Facility / General, Gear / Equipment, or Client / Billing. This is what makes the log searchable later.
3
Add an acknowledgment note
Say what you're doing about it — who's on it, what the plan is, what you're waiting on.
4
Click Acknowledge
Moves the flag from pending to acknowledged so everyone knows it's being handled.
5
When resolved: click Resolve
Fill in what was done (required), the vendor or person if applicable, and the cost if applicable. Then close it out.
Why resolution details matter
The Flags Log is the studio's maintenance history. Vendor names and costs logged here are how you track recurring issues, find reliable contractors, and know what repairs have cost over time. Every resolved flag with complete details makes the log more valuable — a vague resolution is a gap in the record.
Search & filter
The Flags Log has a search bar that searches across runner notes, acknowledgment notes, resolution notes, and vendor names. Filter by status (pending, acknowledged, resolved) and by category to find exactly what you need — every past fix, every vendor, every recurring problem.
Golden rules
✓ Always
✓Flag it immediately — don't wait to see if it resolves itself
✓Be specific in the runner note — vague flags are hard to action
✓Categorize it — categories make the log searchable and useful
✓Fill in resolution details — the log is only as good as what you put in it
✗ Never
✗Sit on a flag hoping it fixes itself
✗Write a vague, one-line note with no location or detail
✗Resolve a flag without saying what was done
✗Let resolved-but-undocumented flags pile up — an open issue is an open issue
