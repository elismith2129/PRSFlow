Runner app guide

Start here

This guide is about the app only — how to use PRSFlo on your phone. The runner manual (the job itself) is a separate thing. Two minutes here and you'll know every screen.

Signing in

Go to prsflow.paramountrecording.com and sign in with your email and password (you got them from the office — they're yours, don't share them). Add the app to your home screen when your phone offers; it works like a regular app after that.?iPhone: open in Safari → Share → "Add to Home Screen." Android: Chrome offers an Install banner.

Your account is you — what you check off, submit, and write is recorded under your name. That's a good thing: it's what proves you did your job.

Picking your studio

First open, you pick a studio. The app remembers it — next time you land straight on your studio's page. The little pills at the top (PRS · ARS · ERS · TRK) switch studios in one tap if you're covering somewhere else, and the ← button forgets your studio so you can pick fresh.

The greeting follows the clock — "this morning," "today," "tonight." The studios run 24/7; the app knows what shift you're on.

Your day in the app

The studio page stacks everything in the order you need it:

1From the office — tasks left for your studio and shift. Check them first; tap the circle when one's done.
2Today's sessions — one card per session, the room letter big. Tap a card to open its work order — that's where your session work happens (next chapter).
3The duty tiles — opening & closing checklists, mic inventory, petty cash, stock. Green "Submitted" means done; quiet text means not yet.
4Shift notes — write the day down as it happens.
5Missed a punch? — at the bottom, takes 30 seconds, same day.
The one habitWork top to bottom. If the office left a task, it's the first thing on your screen because it's the first thing they need.

The work order

Every session has one work order — the session's paperwork. You fill in what actually happened; the office sees it live as you type.

The day cards

The work order shows one card per day. Each card has a status pill:

Not submittedSubmittedApproved

Tap a card to open the day sheet — the handful of things you actually type. (There's also a list view toggle if you prefer the table.)

The day sheet — what you type

Start2:00 PM End2:00 AM OTauto

Times: type them ("10a", "930p", even "1430") or use the ▾ half-hour picker.?The picker opens scrolled to the time already shown, so you're never crawling from 12:00 AM — and double-check AM/PM before you submit. Staff times follow the studio times until you change them. Overtime figures itself out from the clock — if the session ran past what was booked, the OT line appears on its own. You never type OT math.

Also on the sheet: who engineered (1ST) or assisted (2ND) — "TBD" is fine until you know — the song title, and the equipment pills:

SpeakersMicrophoneConsole

Tap a pill to cycle it: green = OK, red = a problem (add a note and a photo — that goes straight to the office as a flag).

Payments (COD sessions): money taken at the desk goes on the work order the moment it's taken — type, amount, memo. The signature pad is at the bottom. A red Balance due chip up top means money is still owed on this session.

What you can't touch: the client block ("Set by the office"), rates, and dates. You can always tap the phone number to call the A&R, even on locked parts.

Submitting — the one rule

When your day is done, hit Submit at the bottom. Something changed after you submitted? Just fix it and hit Update submission — same button, no penalty, ever.?Sessions change near the end all the time. The office wants it CORRECT, not frozen — updating a submission is doing your job well, not admitting a mistake.

You can always fix your day until the office approves it.

Once a day shows Approved, it's locked — the office checked it and signed off. If something's wrong on an approved day, tell the office; they can unlock it.

Long day?If a day runs past 14 hours the app asks "really?" — it warns, it never blocks. Long sessions happen.

Checklists & duties

The four tiles on your studio page. Each one saves as you tap — no save button, no losing work.

Opening & closing checklists

Tap each item as you do it — it saves instantly. Found something wrong? Use needs attention: write what you saw, add a photo. That creates a flag for the manager automatically — you never need to also text someone.?A needs-attention note IS the report. It lands in the manager's morning queue with your name and photo attached.

Why it mattersA checklist that never comes in shows up RED on the manager's morning screen — louder than one with a problem. Submitting with an issue noted is always better than not submitting.

Mic inventory

Every mic gets one of three answers: Here · In a room (say which) · Missing. Marking one missing alerts the office — that's the point, don't hesitate. Counted items (odds & ends) get a quantity. Submit when the sweep's done.

Petty cash & stock

Petty cash: every dollar in or out, as it happens — tap In/Out, amount, what for. Stock: walk the list, mark anything running low. Both take two minutes and both feed the office's morning review.

Shift notes

The old end-of-shift Slack post, but live. Write entries through your shift, as things happen — don't save it all up for the end.

Each entry is stamped with your name and the time. Photos welcome. Handing off mid-shift? The next runner keeps writing in the same day's log — that's the handoff, on the record.

Entries can't be edited or deleted — they're records. Typo'd something important? Write a new entry correcting it.

What a good log looks likeShort lines, as they happen: "11:40 — Session B wrapped, client took roughs." "1:15 — handed off to Mathew." The manager reads this every morning; write what tomorrow will want to know.

Missed a punch

Forgot to clock in or out, or a meal punch didn't take? Report it in the app — bottom of your studio page, takes 30 seconds.

Pick what kind of punch it was (clock in, clock out, meal), the shift date, and the real time. That's it — the report goes to the manager, who fixes it in ADP.

Do it the same day. The report is the written confirmation the law requires before anyone can edit your hours — it protects you, and same-day reports protect you best.?California requires your written confirmation before a time punch is edited. This form IS that confirmation — which is why it's in the app and not a text to the manager.

The rules, in one place

1You can always fix your day until the office approves it. Submit, then update freely — no penalty.
2Record things when they happen — payments, rentals, notes, cash. The paperwork should say what the desk saw, the moment it saw it.
3If something's wrong, say so in the app — needs attention, a flag, a missing mic. Marking a problem is doing the job, not causing trouble.
4Rates, dates, and the client block are the office's. If one looks wrong, call — the number's right there on the work order.
5Submitted-with-a-problem always beats never-submitted. A checklist that never comes in is the loudest alarm on the manager's screen — an honest problem noted is just a normal day.

Words you'll see

Work order — the session's paperwork; one per session. Day sheet — one day's card opened up. Submit / Update submission — turning your day in; same button. Approved — the office checked your day; it's locked. Needs attention — your note + photo about a problem; becomes a flag. Flag — a problem on the record with the office. Shift notes — the day's log, entry by entry. From the office — tasks left for your studio.

Something broken in the app?

A red "NOT saved" message means exactly that — try again, and tell the office if it keeps happening. If this guide doesn't match your screen, the app changed — tell the office so the guide gets fixed.
