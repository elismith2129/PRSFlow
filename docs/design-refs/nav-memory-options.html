Moving between pages without losing your place

Staff: billing hub → calendar for a quick look → back, and you're on the front page or the default view with the rail group closed. Four pieces below. A fixes the actual fault; B and D are small and sit on top of A; C is the bigger add and can wait.

A · Pages remember where you were

Recommended · do first

Each page writes its position into the address: which view, which bins, which week. Three things then happen for free — the browser's Back button really goes back, the rail's Billing link returns you to the view you last had open, and the rail keeps the right group open because it follows the page instead of a click. Nothing new to learn. No visible change except that the app stops forgetting.

What each page carries
  • Billing hub — view (invoices / financials / tenants), open bins, month if Financials.
  • Calendar — week or day shown, room if filtered.
  • CRM — filter (hot / warm / uncontacted / all), open lead if a card is open.
  • Daily ops — already does this (?tab=flags). The pattern exists; this spreads it.
  • Dashboard, Petty cash, SRS — nothing to carry.
Rail behaviour
  • Group opens when you are on one of its pages. Stays open while you are there.
  • Billing in the rail goes to your last billing address, not the bare page. Same for CRM and Calendar.
  • Remembered per device, so the iPad at the desk and Lori's laptop each keep their own.

B · The return pill

Recommended · with A

When you arrive on a page from another page, a small pill at the top says where you came from and takes you straight back. It is A's remembered address with a label on it — the "how do I get back" moment stops being a hunt through the rail. Disappears after you use it or go somewhere else. On the phone it sits under the header, full width, where the thumb lands.

D · Open in a new window

Recommended · with A

Your addition, and the honest answer to "tabs": staff already have browser tabs. The reason they don't feel useful today is A — a new tab opens on the bare page. With A in place, every rail link, return pill and record opens in a new window at the exact view. Three places to put it, all small. ⌘-click already works on every link in the app today; this just makes it visible.

C · Peek at the calendar without leaving

Later · if still asked for after A

Your exact example. A button on the billing hub (and the CRM) slides the calendar's week over the page. You look, you close it, and you are exactly where you were because you never left. Nothing to remember because nothing moved. The bigger build of the four — the calendar has to work as a panel as well as a page — and it only helps the look-but-don't-touch case; the moment someone needs to edit a booking they are on the calendar page anyway, where A and B take over.

The pick
  • A + B + D in one build. A is the repair, B makes the repair visible, D is one line per link once A exists.
  • C after, only if staff still ask. Expect A alone to take most of the complaint away.
  • Not building in-app tabs. Pages would stay mounted together (realtime channels, iPad memory) and the browser already has tabs — D points at them.
What it costs / what to watch
  • No migration. Addresses and one per-device memory; nothing in the database.
  • Billing page: view and bins move from React state into the address. Same for the calendar's date and the CRM filter. Each page is a contained change.
  • Rail: "open group follows the page" replaces "open group follows the click" — the Oct 12 single-page-group rule stays.
  • Return pill is one level deep by design. A trail (billing → calendar → lead → back → back) is a breadcrumb, and breadcrumbs are what the rail is for.
