Chapter 01

You own the money side

PRSFlo is two worlds. Operations is the studio manager's — the building, the runners, the nightly checklists. Billing is yours — work orders, invoices, payments, money owed. Nothing in your world lives anywhere else, and nothing from the other world will chase you. One item, one home.

The one rule that shapes everything

PRSFlo owns the workflow and the documents. QuickBooks owns the money.?Quick test: "where is this invoice?" → PRSFlo. "How much did we make in July?" → QuickBooks. PRSFlo tells you what needs invoicing, keeps the work order and the invoice PDF together, and always knows where every invoice sits. QuickBooks is still the accounting — the invoice itself is created there, and the books live there.

Why it worksThe two systems track different things, so they never fight. There's no "which one is right" — PRSFlo is right about where things are, QuickBooks is right about the money.

COD clients

Pay at the desk, during or right after the session. The money is already in by the time you see the work order — your job is to check it's accurate and attach the invoice. Minutes per session. Overtime gets collected at the desk, in the moment.

Billing clients

Labels, and anyone invoiced after the fact. The full cycle: review → invoice → owner approval → send → chase → paid. Days to weeks per invoice.

Where you'll live

Three screens, all in the left rail. Dashboard — the morning read; Flo's briefing tells you what needs you. My Day — your recurring duties, as a card that tracks itself. Billing — the hub; every work order and invoice sorted by what it's waiting for. The next chapter follows one session all the way through them.

Chapter 02 · The flow

A session, start to finish

This is why the app is called Flo. Every session moves down one line — from first hold to money in the bank — and every screen you use is just a window onto a different part of that line. Learn this chapter and the rest of the manual is detail.

1
Front desk / management · Calendar

It starts with a session

A lead becomes a hold, a hold becomes a session on the calendar. You don't book sessions — but the moment one exists, it's already on its way to you. A tentative hold isn't yours yet; a confirmed session is where your clock starts.

Tentative — a hold, not yours yet Confirmed — your clock starts
2
PRSFlo · automatic

The work order is born with it

Every session gets one work order, created automatically the moment it's booked — nobody makes one by hand, and there's never a session without one or two for one session. It gets a work order number, and it's the single container for everything billable: studio time, engineer time, rentals, overtime, payments.

WO-1042 One session · one work order
The idea to internalizeThe work order IS the session's paperwork. The calendar and the work order aren't two things — the calendar is just where the work order shows its dates.
3
You · QuickBooks + the hub

Confirmed means an invoice exists

The second a session confirms, create its invoice in QuickBooks. That way the invoice number exists from day one and is referenced on the work order — every document about this session carries both numbers from the start. The amounts get trued up later; the number is what matters now.

WO-1042INV-2214 Created at confirmation, updated after review?Quote both numbers in any email about a session — either one finds it instantly, in PRSFlo or in QuickBooks.
Why so earlyA session that runs before its invoice exists is a session you're chasing paperwork for afterwards. Number first, money later — the flow never has a document with no reference.
4
You · the work order

Set the work order up before it runs

Open the work order (double-click its row in the hub) and set what the office knows: studio rates, engineer rates, expected rentals — and for billing clients, the PO number, or mark "No PO needed." Do it now: nothing can be approved later until PO-or-none is noted, and this is the cheapest moment to know.

Rates — office-set, runners can't touch PO noted or No-PO ticked
5
Runners · their phones, live

The session runs — the work order fills itself in

During the session the runner works the same work order from their phone: real start and end times, who engineered, equipment condition, song titles — and for COD, payments taken at the desk. Overtime computes itself from the clock (past the booked end on hourly days, past 12h on lockouts), so it can't be typed wrong or double-billed.

When their day is done they submit it. Submitted is a signal, not a seal — a runner can keep correcting their day until the office approves it, and everything they type appears on your screen live.

● Submitted — runner says it's done ● Approved — office checked it, now it's locked
6
You · next morning

The morning after: review

Last night's sessions are waiting in the hub. Double-click each one and check the runner's story against reality: times per day (a wrong end time makes a wrong OT), rates, rentals, payments recorded. Fix anything wrong — your edits land live, including on a runner's phone. Approve each day as you verify it (that's the lock), then Complete the work order. Missing times block completion — the app tells you exactly what's unfinished.

In QuickBooks, true up INV-2214 to the reviewed totals and export the PDF. Then attach it the easy way: drag the PDF straight onto the work order's row in the hub and drop it. The row highlights as you hover, the invoice attaches, the Invoiced light turns on, and the row routes itself to its next stop — no upload screen, no filing. (The Attach invoice button does the same thing through a file picker if you'd rather click.) From here the work order and its invoice travel together as one package.

Reviewed Invoiced Approved
7
You + an owner · the hub

Out the door, and back with money

Billing side: an owner (Eli or Adam-Mike) approves the package — the PO gate holds it until PO-or-none is on the work order. Then two acts, in order: Download builds the merged invoice + work order PDF; you email it; Mark sent confirms it actually left. From that day it ages. At 31+ days it's your chase list. When the cheque or ACH lands: Mark paid — that button is the only record on our side that the money arrived, so press it the day it does.

COD side: the money came in at the desk, so there's no approval and nothing to send. Check it, attach the invoice, done. If collection was missed, the session lands in Balance due — rare, and the most important thing on the page when it happens.

DownloadMark sent?Press Mark sent the day it actually leaves — aging starts from this moment, so batching it "for Friday" makes every invoice look younger than it is. Mark paidBalance due — collection missed

The whole flow in one line

session confirmed → WO-1042 born → INV-2214 created → WO set up (rates·PO) → runners fill it live → you review + approve days → owner approves → download → sent → aging → paid

Who can touch what

The office (you) — everything: rates, dates, rooms, rows, batch edits, approval, Complete.

Runners — the things that happen at the desk: times, staff names and roles, OT that follows from times, equipment condition, song titles, payments taken, session notes, the COD signature. And rentals — a rental added at the desk is a real event.

Runners can't touch — the client block ("set by the office"), any rate, row dates (the schedule is the office's), or an approved day. They see rates as read-only text; the schedule they can only report against, not rewrite.

The principleWhoever witnesses a fact records it. Runners witness time, condition, and cash; the office owns identity, price, and schedule. Every edit lands live on the other side's screen either way.

Read this screen like thisThe four figures are clickable — each jumps to the tab it counts. The three lights are the assembly line (Reviewed · Invoiced · Approved). And every row shows at most one button — always the next action. You never choose from a menu; the app knows where the package is and asks for the one thing that moves it. Double-clicking a row opens the document itself — the work order, or once the invoice is attached, both combined.

The COD side of the toggle

Balance due · 1Needs review · 3Paid

Balance due leads its side, always. A COD session where collection was missed is the only way COD goes wrong — rare, critical, and never allowed to hide behind settled sessions. The balance is collected and recorded on the work order itself. Needs review is your normal COD morning: money's in, check the work order, attach the invoice, done.

Guardrails you'll notice (they're features)

Editing an approved package un-approves it, by itself. An owner approved $4,180 — not "whatever this becomes." Change the numbers and it quietly returns to needs-approval.

The drift flag. If a work order changes after its invoice was attached, the row flags that billed ≠ owed. Better to hear it from the app than from the client. A sent invoice never silently un-approves — use Pull it back to deliberately restart the cycle.

The stale-download reminder. Downloaded but not marked sent within 2 days goes hot — the nudge that keeps a finished package from dying in your Downloads folder.

Cancelled sessions with a live invoice don't vanish. The client still owes the money — void it properly through Closed, with a reason on the record.

Reading the cardThe small number wells are captured counts — type the real number when you complete the duty (transactions cleared, invoices updated, COD outstanding). They're not busywork: they're what Flo's briefing and Eli's oversight numbers are built from, until the QuickBooks connection can compute them automatically.

The rules the card lives by

Day-scoped duties only show on their day. Monday's report shows Monday; rent shows on the 25th. Friday's work never clutters Monday's list — the day-before heads-up comes from Flo instead.

Missed days accrue, honestly. Miss Ramp two days and the duty doesn't duplicate — one line says "covering 3 days," and one checkmark clears the whole backlog, because you did catch up on all of it.

Monthly duties escalate instead. Miss rent on the 25th and it stays put, red, counting the days — the next natural chance is four weeks away and rent can't wait for it.

Never "late" on the day itself. At 9am, today's unfinished duties are just today's work. Late means a previous day went unfinished.

Anyone can cover. If you're out, another admin can work your card — the record shows who actually did each item. And unchecking a mistake is allowed; history is kept either way.

Chapter 07

The Flo briefing

The Flo box reads like a note from an assistant. It is not AI and not guessing — every line is computed from real records by fixed rules. Once you know the rules you can trust it completely, and you'll know exactly what to do about every line it shows you.

Flo· Your briefing · Aug 28?No AI here — every line is a fixed rule over live data. If a line surprises you, the data changed, not the rules.
Tenant rent invoices were due Mon 8/25 — 3 days late, needs doing ASAP
RED — something slipped. Appears for exactly three reasons: a duty backlog hits 3+ days · a monthly duty blew its date (this one — loudest, because the next chance is a month away) · a duty due yesterday wasn't done.
2 sessions missing work orders
3 balances outstanding · $5,940
AMBER — pressure building, not failure. Counted fresh every morning from the live queues: sessions that should have a work order and don't, work orders where owed exceeds taken, and the COD numbers you typed on your card.
You cleared all 5 duties yesterday
GREEN — earned, not decorative. Only appears when every duty due yesterday was actually done.
Tomorrow: Ramp weekly report
TOMORROW — the heads-up that day-scoped duties give up by only showing on their own day. Weekly and monthly duties only; never repeats something already red.
"One thing needs you today — start with the rent invoices."

What clearing something means

Red clearsby doing the duty — check it off on your My Day card and the whole backlog it names is covered. There is no dismiss and no snooze, on purpose.
Amber clearsitself, when reality improves. Create the missing work orders and that line vanishes; collect the balances and that one goes. You never tick anything.
Green & Tomorrowaren't clearable — one is a report, the other a heads-up.
The mental modelFlo is a mirror, not an inbox. Nothing in it needs "processing," and every line disappears exactly when the thing it describes stops being true.

What Flo will never do

Never accuses you of history before your time — day one starts from zero. Never marks today's unfinished work late. Never says the same thing twice in one briefing. And never shows you another role's chores — you get your world; Eli gets the cross-role view.

Chapter 09

Help & words

When something looks wrong

A number that can't be right — open the work order and read the rows. Every total is computed from them, so the answer is always in the rows.

A red "NOT saved" message — the save failed and the app is telling you honestly. Try again; if it keeps happening, tell Eli — errors are logged and he can see them.

The manual doesn't match the screen — the app changed and the manual didn't. Say so; this document is meant to be corrected, and the version history (bottom of the rail) says what changed in each release.

Words this manual uses

Work order (WO) — one session's billable paperwork; born with the booking; numbered. · COD — paid at the desk. Billing — invoiced after. · Complete / Reviewed — you checked the WO; it's accurate. · Approve a day — locking a runner's day sheet after verifying it. · Approve an invoice — an owner signing off the package. Owners only. · Package — the merged invoice + work order PDF that goes to the client. · Drift — the WO changed after invoicing; billed ≠ owed. · Duty — a recurring My Day item. Backlog — its missed prior days. · Queue — a computed list (needs-WO, balances); empties itself. · Flo — the briefing; computed, rule-based, no AI.
