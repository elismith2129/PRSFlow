The Lakers game pop-up, three ways

Same content in each: the game at the top, who was offered, who got the pair, the two claims. The question is how much of it gets a container. Dark is the default register; flip to light to check the mirror.

The one rule all three test: a rounded fill is for a thing you can press, or a row you can open. Information itself sits straight on the surface, and type does the grouping: a tracked micro-label above, a bigger weight for the fact, muted ink for the trailing detail.

Inputs are the biggest source of bubbles today, because every field is a well whether or not you're in it. In all three options a field is plain text until you click it; the wash fill appears only under the field you're editing, then goes away. One bubble at a time, and it's the one you're looking at.

Today's pop-up, for reference, has nine wells, two chip rows and a suggestion strip inside a 480px card. It is shown first.

How it works
  • "Went to" is one field. Type a name; the company comes with the match and sits after it in muted ink. If you just typed a name, it stands alone.
  • "Offered" is a list, one name per line. Enter adds the next line. Every name here is someone who passed; that's what the column means, so there's no second state to show.

Prose — the card reads as sentences

Fill in the blanks. There are no labels at all; the sentence is the label. The least chrome of the three, and the most different from the rest of the app.

How it works
  • Each bold phrase is a field. Click it, it gets the wash fill and a caret (shown on Ela). Click away, the fill goes.
  • An empty blank is muted text that says what goes there: "+ another", "Add one".
  • The sentence changes with the data: "Nobody was offered yet" / "Not given yet". The grammar is the empty state.
  • Company appears automatically from the client match ("at Interscope"); if you typed a plain name it just ends at the name.
Risks
  • Nothing else in PRSFlo reads like this, so it won't travel to tables or the WO. It fits small single-record cards: this one, a flag, a task, a memo.
  • Fields that aren't in a sentence (a date) need a sentence invented for them.

If one of these goes site-wide, this is the rule it writes

  • A fill means pressable or openable. Buttons, pills, list rows that open something, the one field you're editing. Nothing else.
  • Reading is done by type, not by boxes. Micro-label above, the fact at 14px, the hero fact bigger, the trailing detail in muted ink at the right edge.
  • A field is text at rest. The wash well appears on focus and leaves on blur. Forms stop looking like forms until you touch them.
  • Chips are for things you can press. A name that was offered and passed is a line with a strike, not a chip. A result in a dropdown is a row, not a chip.
  • Where it would go next, in order of how little it risks: the task and flag modals, the lead profile, the client card, the WO's session-info block. Tables and the calendar stay as they are.
