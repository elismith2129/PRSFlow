Day sheet — where OT comes from

All three share the rule: OT is time beyond what's included — a lockout includes 12 hours, an hourly day includes the hours the office booked. The runner only ever touches the clock; hours and dollars are computed. The options argue how much confirmation sits on top. 1 is fully automatic (zero taps, zero judgment). 2 derives the OT and asks the runner to tap-confirm or waive it — one tap, judgment recorded, still nothing typed. 3 prefills an editable well and warns hot when the typed value disagrees with the clock. Phones 1–2 are an hourly day that ran 3h past the booked end; phone 3 is a lockout. The billing block in each shows the engineer/assistant rate's new home — an input for the office (shown), read-only for the runner.
